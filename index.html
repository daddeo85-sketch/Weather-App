The big picture: highs, lows and fronts

H is high pressure: sinking, drier air and fair weather. L is low pressure: rising air that builds clouds and rain.
Cold front (blue, triangles): colder air shoving in, often with a line of storms, then cooler, drier air behind it.
Warm front (red, half circles): warm, humid air sliding in, with steady rain ahead of it.
Stalled front (red and blue): a front that stopped moving. Over Florida these park and soak us for days.

Air flows clockwise out of a high and counterclockwise into a low. A big high to our north pushes a steady northeast wind off the ocean, which piles moisture onto the coast.

      What the tide types mean

      High and low tide: about every 6 hours the water rises to a high, then drops to a low. Fort Pierce gets two of each a day.

      Spring tides: around the new and full moon the sun and moon pull in a line, so highs run higher and lows run lower. Nothing to do with the season.

      Neap tides: around the half moons the pulls work against each other, so the water barely moves between high and low.

      King tides: a spring tide when the moon is also at its closest to Earth (perigee). These are the year's highest tides and can flood low streets and docks, especially in the fall and with an onshore wind.

      Tide times are NOAA predictions. Wind and rain can push the real water higher than predicted. King, spring and neap dates are worked out from the moon's position, and the effect usually shows up a day or two after the date.

      Full moons ahead

        What the moon names mean

        Month names (Wolf, Snow, Worm, Pink, Flower, Strawberry, Buck, Sturgeon, Harvest, Hunter's, Beaver, Cold): old names for each month's full moon. The Harvest Moon is the full moon closest to the first day of fall, and the Hunter's Moon is the one after it.

        Supermoon: a full moon when the moon is near its closest to Earth. It looks a bit bigger and brighter, and it brings king tides.

        Micromoon: a full moon near its farthest point. It looks a little smaller.

        Blue moon: the second full moon in one calendar month. It isn't actually blue.

        Black moon: the second new moon in one month. There's nothing to see, since a new moon is dark.

        Blood moon: a total lunar eclipse. Earth's shadow covers the moon and turns it a coppery red. A partial eclipse takes a dark bite out of the moon, and a penumbral one only shades it faintly.

        Moon phases and distances are calculated in the app. Eclipse dates are NASA's (Fred Espenak's eclipse tables). NASA lunar eclipse list
